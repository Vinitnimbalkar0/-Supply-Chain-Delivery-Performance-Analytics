# Insights

## Executive Summary

The analysis indicates that **shipping mode is a much stronger observed delivery-risk signal than market**.

The largest management opportunity is therefore to investigate high-risk shipping modes while considering revenue exposure and profitability.

---

## INS-01 — First Class Has the Highest Observed Late Rate

First Class has an observed late-delivery rate of approximately **97.4%**.

| Shipping Mode | Approx. Late Rate |
|---|---:|
| Standard Class | 38.0% |
| Same Day | 48.2% |
| Second Class | 79.0% |
| First Class | 97.4% |

### Implication

First Class should be the first shipping mode investigated operationally.

This is an association, not proof that First Class causes delays.

---

## INS-02 — Second Class Also Shows Elevated Risk

Second Class has an observed late rate of approximately **79%**, substantially above the overall delivery-risk level.

### Implication

Second Class should also be included in the operational review.

---

## INS-03 — Market-Level Rates Are Relatively Narrow

Approximate market late rates range from **55.5% to 59.2%**.

This is much narrower than the variation across shipping modes.

### Implication

Geography alone does not explain the largest observed delivery-performance differences.

---

## INS-04 — First Class Risk Persists Across Markets

Approximate First Class late rates:

| Market | First Class Late Rate |
|---|---:|
| Africa | 99.3% |
| Pacific Asia | 97.8% |
| Europe | 97.3% |
| USCA | 97.3% |
| LATAM | 96.7% |

### Implication

The pattern appears across markets and should be investigated at the fulfillment/process level.

---

## INS-05 — Europe and LATAM Have the Largest Sales Exposure

Approximate market sales:

| Market | Sales |
|---|---:|
| Europe | 4.60M |
| LATAM | 4.22M |
| Pacific Asia | 3.02M |
| USCA | 1.57M |
| Africa | 0.70M |

### Implication

High-revenue markets should receive priority when elevated delivery risk is also present.

---

## INS-06 — Product Risk Requires Business Context

Product/category rankings should combine:

```text
Orders
+
Sales
+
Late Rate
+
Shipping Variance
```

A small product with a high late rate should not automatically receive more management attention than a high-value product with a moderately elevated rate.

---

## INS-07 — Trend Analysis Should Combine Volume and Risk

Monthly analysis should monitor:

- Orders
- Sales
- Late rate
- Shipping variance
- Shipping-mode mix

Periods where both demand and late rate increase should be investigated as potential operational pressure periods.

---

## INS-08 — Late-Order Sales Are Revenue Exposure, Not Proven Revenue Loss

Sales associated with late-delivery orders should be described as **revenue exposure**.

The dataset does not provide enough evidence to claim that those sales were lost.

---

## INS-09 — Financial Prioritization Should Combine Risk and Profitability

The most important areas are those with:

```text
High Late Rate
+
High Sales Exposure
+
Weak Profitability
```

This is more useful than ranking solely by late rate.

---

## INS-10 — Discounts Need a Profitability Lens

Discounts should be evaluated through:

```text
Discount
→ Sales
→ Profit
→ Margin
```

Higher sales do not automatically mean better performance if profitability deteriorates.

---

## Executive Takeaway

> **Prioritize delivery improvement where operational risk intersects with meaningful revenue exposure and weak profitability.**
